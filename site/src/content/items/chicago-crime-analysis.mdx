## Problem

Which crimes are most common in Chicago, where do they happen, and how have they changed in recent years?

## Data

The City of Chicago's public crime dataset: 7,930,875 records with 22 columns, which I cut down to 877,453 records
from 2020 to 2023, with the 9 columns the analysis needed.

<More>
- Kept 2020 to 2023, so the analysis reflects the city today.
- Dropped columns the questions did not need, then removed duplicates and incomplete rows.
- Split each date into day, month and year, and added a time-of-day bucket, so patterns over the day and the year
  could be studied.
</More>

## Approach

<Flow
  label="How the team's analysis was organised"
  steps={['Prepare and clean the data', 'Rank crime types', 'Top five by district and community area', 'Trends by year and month', 'PCA and simple classifiers']}
/>

I laid out the analysis section by section in a shared R Markdown document, with each part assigned, so four people
could work in parallel and the results would fit together.

<More>
- **My sections:** data preparation, the ranking of crime types by share (with a pie chart and a top-five bar chart),
  and arrest rates for the top five crime types.
- **Teammates' sections:** crime by district and community area, trends over the years and months, PCA, and k-nearest
  neighbours and random-forest classifiers.
</More>

## Results

Theft, battery, criminal damage, assault and motor vehicle theft were the five most common crimes, and motor vehicle
theft rose sharply after 2021.

## My part

- I led the team: I set up the repository, wrote the analysis plan that divided the work, and finalised the
  presentation.
- I wrote the data preparation and the crime-type analysis.

<More>
The plan was a skeleton of the whole report, with a section for each question and notes on what each teammate would
add, set up before any analysis began. The team presented the results on 21 April 2024.
</More>
