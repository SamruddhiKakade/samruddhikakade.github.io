## Context

In a course scenario, a company wanted to know whether to develop "Game-X", a role-playing game based on an anime it
held the rights to, and whether to charge a fee to download it. The report had to give the company's chief operating
officer a clear answer and the reasons for it.

## Approach

The work split into four research tasks: the market and competitor games, the target audience, the costs and revenue,
and whether the game's technical features would be acceptable to players. A final task combined the findings into one
recommendation.

<More label="Sources, report structure and the document series">
The report drew on published articles about the market and comparable games. It followed the structure a
decision-maker expects: a cover memo, an abstract, an executive summary, the research methods, the results,
conclusions and the recommendation, with references. It was the last of three documents written during the course: a
proposal, a progress report and then this recommendation report.
</More>

## Recommendation

Proceed with the game and drop the planned download fee, relying on in-game purchases instead. The report predicted
that in-game purchases would earn the majority of the profit, and judged that a one-off download fee could reduce those
earnings.

<More label="Evidence and cost comparison">
Two points supported the recommendation: an industry article reporting that games without a download fee but with
in-game purchases earned more than games that charged both, and players being less willing to spend on in-game items in
a game they had already bought. The report compared one download fee of about 0.60 million with about 0.25 million of
profit each month from in-game purchases, after about 0.24 million of monthly running costs. These are the report's
own estimates for the course scenario.
</More>

## Findings and limitations

The report found that anime-based role-playing and adventure games were a rising genre, that the likely audience was
aged 18 to 35 and mostly anime fans, and that the proposed features were acceptable to those players.

<More label="Limits of the evidence">
The report is a course exercise. Its market evidence came largely from published articles, and its financial figures
are estimates without a shown calculation. It assessed whether players would accept the features, not how the game
would be engineered.
</More>
