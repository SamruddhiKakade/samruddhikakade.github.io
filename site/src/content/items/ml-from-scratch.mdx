## Context

Three CS 584 Machine Learning assignments (Fall 2023). Two asked for the core methods to be written out rather than
called from a library: logistic and softmax regression trained by gradient descent, and a perceptron for handwritten
digits. The third compared linear, ridge and lasso regression on car data. Scores are read against simple baselines,
because the datasets are small.

## Approach

- **Logistic and softmax regression:** sigmoid, softmax and cross-entropy loss in NumPy, trained by batch gradient
  descent and by mini-batch stochastic gradient descent on a ten-point, three-class table.
- **Perceptron:** one layer with ten outputs, in NumPy, comparing linear, sigmoid and ReLU activations on scikit-learn's
  1,797 8×8 handwritten digits (1,078 training, 359 validation and 360 test images, stratified by digit).
- **Regularisation:** linear, ridge and lasso regression with scikit-learn on the UCI Automobile data (205 cars),
  predicting city fuel economy from five measurements, with and without degree-5 polynomial features. A 60/20/20 split
  chose the regularisation strength on validation data.

<More label="Update rules and library use">
Logistic regression predicts $\hat y = \sigma(w^\top x + b)$ with $\sigma(z) = 1/(1+e^{-z})$ and updates
$w \leftarrow w - \eta\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_i(\hat y_i - y_i)\,x_i$ on the cross-entropy loss. Softmax regression uses
$\hat y_k = e^{z_k} / \sum_j e^{z_j}$ with the same form of step for each class. Mini-batch descent takes each step on a
batch of two points instead of all ten.

In the digits assignment, scikit-learn supplied only the data, the split, the one-hot encoding and the metrics; the
weight updates are NumPy code.
</More>

## Contribution

- Implemented logistic and softmax regression in NumPy, with batch and mini-batch gradient descent, and compared the
  two optimisers by loss and F1 score over 1,000 iterations.
- Implemented a single-layer perceptron in NumPy with linear, sigmoid and ReLU activations, and evaluated it on 360
  held-out digit images.
- Compared linear, ridge and lasso regression, with and without degree-5 polynomial features, selecting the
  regularisation strength on a validation split.

## Findings and limitations

The perceptron with ReLU activation classified 341 of 360 held-out digit images correctly (94.7%), against 10.3% for
always predicting the most frequent digit. Sigmoid reached 94.2% and the linear activation 88.6%.

On the car data, linear regression reached R² = 0.73 on 41 held-out cars (test MSE 10.54). Degree-5 polynomial
features lowered the test MSE to 5.25 with ridge regression and 5.11 with lasso, a test R² of 0.87.

<More label="Limitations of the experiments">
- The perceptron results come from one run with random initial weights. ReLU and sigmoid differ by two images out of
  360, so their order is not established. The learning rate (0.0001) and the 30 epochs were not tuned.
- With the linear activation the cross-entropy loss is undefined, because outputs can be negative, so its accuracy is
  taken from the largest output and no loss curve is reported.
- On the five original car measurements, no regularisation gave the lowest validation error, so ridge and lasso matched
  plain linear regression there. Without regularisation, the degree-5 features (252 of them, for 123 training cars)
  left ridge regression unstable.
- The regression test set has 41 cars, and the logistic and softmax models were scored on the same ten points they
  were trained on, so those figures show optimisation behaviour, not generalisation.
</More>
