## Context

The course exercise was to prototype a race-to-the-end board game. The aim was a game in which strategy counts
alongside the roll of the die, suitable for families with children over twelve.

<More label="Audience and theme choice">
Strategy set the age range: the game suits families, but its strategy is most enjoyable for children over twelve. Two
themes were tried and dropped before the final one.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Theme | Kept or dropped | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Escape from zombies | Dropped | Too dark for a family game |
| Capture the flag | Dropped | Did not suit a race to the finish, and the mechanics were too tedious |
| Treasure race | Kept | Fits a race, and was inspired by a Hindi-language comedy film about a group racing to a hidden treasure |

</div>
</More>

## Approach

Two to four players race along a path to the treasure. Players set traps that hold back their rivals and shortcuts
that move themselves forward. Each player draws a character card that changes how the game plays for them, and the
first player to reach the treasure wins.

<figure>
  <Image src={cards} alt="The four character cards: Trap Designer, Map Expert, Just Lucky and The Strategist, each with its ability written on it." width={720} />
  <figcaption class="muted small">The four character cards, each with its own ability.</figcaption>
</figure>

<More label="Rules and characters">
Before the race, players take turns placing one or two cards on the board until every card is used. Traps affect only
rivals and shortcuts affect only the player who set them. After that, a turn is either a die roll or a move of one of
the player's placed cards to a new space. Each character receives a different mix of trap and shortcut cards.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Character | Ability |
| --- | --- |
| Trap Designer | Can strengthen their traps by up to 2 spaces |
| Map Expert | Can strengthen their shortcuts by up to 2 spaces |
| Just Lucky | Can avoid three traps |
| The Strategist | Extra turns, never two in a row |

</div>

An optional ending, "The last stretch", requires an exact roll to land on the treasure.
</More>

Playtesting changed the design in two ways:

- **A loophole:** if every player used only traps, the game dragged on and broke down. Characters were added so that
  each player receives a different mix of traps and shortcuts, and their strengths and card counts were revised many
  times until all four were roughly equal in strength.
- **The last stretch:** in one playtest a very lucky player stayed far ahead even when the others combined their traps.
  A playtester suggested requiring the exact number on the die to reach the treasure, so that the leader could be
  caught. After more playtesting it was added as a variant.

font chosen to suit the theme, so that players feel they have found the old documents that started the race.

## Contribution

- Designed the game and its rules as an individual course exercise: the trap and shortcut mechanic, four characters
  and the optional exact-roll ending.
- Playtested the game and revised the characters and card counts until the four were roughly equal in strength.
- Illustrated the board, character cards, tokens and rulebook in Canva, in one old-document style.

## Lessons learned

- A rule can work for one strategy and fail when every player adopts it. Playtests in which everyone used the same
  approach exposed the loophole.
- Playtesters' suggestions solved a problem the design had not anticipated: the exact-roll ending came from a
  playtester's idea, and it let trailing players catch a runaway leader.
- Testing themes early saved effort: two themes were dropped after short trials.
