## Problem

Make a race-to-the-finish game where strategy matters as much as the roll of a die.

## How it plays

Two to four players race along the path to the treasure. Before the race, players take turns placing trap cards, which
only hurt their rivals, and shortcut cards, which only help themselves. On each turn a player either rolls the die or
moves one of their own cards. The first to reach the treasure wins.

</figure>

<More>
Each player also has a character that changes how the game plays for them:

- **Trap Designer:** stronger traps.
- **Map Expert:** stronger shortcuts.
- **Lucky:** can ignore three traps.
- **Strategist:** three extra turns, never two in a row.

An optional ending, "The last stretch", asks for an exact roll to land on the treasure.
</More>

## Approach

I tried three themes before settling on a treasure race, then tested the game with players and changed the design
after each round. The board went through three versions.

<More>
An escape-from-zombies theme felt too dark for a family game, and a capture-the-flag theme became tedious in play. The
treasure race kept the tension of a race while leaving room for the trap-and-shortcut strategy. The game is aimed at
families with children over twelve, and I made the art in Canva: an old-scroll map, a parchment rulebook and a themed
font.
</More>

## Results

Playtesting showed a problem my first design had missed: when everyone played only traps, the game stalled. I fixed
it by adding the four characters, each with its own mix of traps and shortcuts, and rebalanced them until each had a
fair chance of winning.

The exact-roll ending came from a playtester's suggestion, and became the "Last stretch" variant in the rulebook.

## My part

I designed the rules, ran the playtests and made all the art in Canva.
