## Context

Two experiments from the CS 581 programming assignments, one on search and one on decision-making under uncertainty:

1. Find a short round trip through 40 campus locations, starting and ending at one campus location.
2. Learn which of 11 Wi-Fi channels is least likely to be busy, from real measurements.

## Approach

For the tour, simulated annealing starts from a random tour and repeatedly reverses a section of it (a two-edge swap).
It accepts a longer tour with a probability that falls as the search cools, so early moves can escape poor tours. For
the Wi-Fi channels, an epsilon-greedy bandit learns on the first part of a trace, then commits to its best channel for
the rest.

<More label="Data and scoring">
- **Tour:** the coordinates of 40 campus locations on a grid. A tour's cost is the sum of straight-line distances
  between consecutive stops, in the grid units of the input file, which carry no physical scale.
- **Wi-Fi:** real traces from a campus library, with signal strength on 11 channels over 5,128 time steps. A channel
  counts as free when its signal is below −90 dBm.
- **Bandit score:** the share of the remaining time steps, after training, on which the chosen channel was free.
</More>

<More label="Acceptance rule, cooling schedule and bandit update">
Simulated annealing accepts a worse tour, longer by $\Delta$, with probability

$$
P(\text{accept}) = e^{-\Delta / T_k}, \qquad T_k = T_0\, e^{-\alpha k}
$$

so a high temperature early on allows exploration, and the search settles as the temperature falls. A smaller $\alpha$
cools more slowly. A run stops when the temperature becomes so small that the computer rounds it to zero.

The epsilon-greedy bandit explores with probability $\varepsilon$, trying a random channel, and otherwise exploits,
choosing the channel that has been free most often so far. A channel earns a reward of 1 when it is free and 0 when it
is busy, and only the channel played is observed.
</More>

<More label="Parameter sweeps and repeated runs">
- Tour: three starting temperatures (100, 1,000 and 10,000) and three cooling rates (α = 0.9, 0.8 and 0.4), each
  setting run five times (45 runs).
- Bandit: nine values of epsilon (0.1 to 0.9) and five training shares (10% to 50% of the trace), each pairing run
  three times (135 runs).
</More>

## Contribution

- Implemented simulated annealing for the 40-stop tour, with two-edge-swap moves and exponential cooling.
- Implemented an epsilon-greedy bandit that estimated each Wi-Fi channel's success rate from a training share of the
  trace and evaluated the chosen channel on the remaining time steps.
- Designed the parameter sweeps, ran 45 tour runs and 135 bandit runs, and reported averages, best runs and spread.

## Findings and limitations

The slowest cooling gave the shortest tours. With a starting temperature of 1,000, the average tour cost was 62.41 at
α = 0.4 against 77.08 at α = 0.9, and the best run reached 60.06. For the bandit, with half the trace for training,
the chosen channel was free on 99.32% of the remaining time steps, within 0.02 percentage points of the best fixed
channel on the same steps (99.34%).

<More label="Limitations">
- Slower cooling also meant more moves, because a run stops only when the temperature rounds to zero: about 1,860 moves at
  α = 0.4 against about 830 at α = 0.9. The comparison does not separate the cooling rate from the number of moves.
- Each tour setting has five runs and each bandit pairing three, with no fixed random seed, so small differences
  between settings are not reliable. Across epsilon values the mean success ranged from 97.8% to 99.3% with no clear
  trend.
- The tour uses one set of 40 locations, and the bandit one library trace and one threshold (−90 dBm). The bandit
  assumes that channels behave later as they did during training.
</More>
