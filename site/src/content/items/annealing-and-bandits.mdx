## Problem

1. Find a short round trip through 40 locations on campus, starting and ending at one building.
2. Learn which of 11 Wi-Fi channels is least likely to be busy, from real measurements.

## Data

- **Tour:** the coordinates of 40 campus locations.
- **Wi-Fi:** real traces from a campus library: signal strength on 11 channels over 5,128 time steps. A channel
  counts as free when its signal is below −90 dBm.

## Approach

For the tour, I implemented simulated annealing: start from a random tour, try swapping two edges, and sometimes accept
a worse tour early on so the search does not get stuck. For the Wi-Fi channels, I implemented an epsilon-greedy
bandit that learns on the first part of a trace and then commits to its best channel for the rest.

<More>
- Each simulated-annealing setting was run five times, over three starting temperatures and three cooling rates.
- The bandit was run over nine values of epsilon (0.1 to 0.9) and five training shares (10% to 50% of the trace),
  three times each.
- I wrote a plotting function to show each bandit run's cumulative success over time.
</More>

## Calculations

Simulated annealing accepts a worse tour, longer by $\Delta$, with probability

$$
P(\text{accept}) = e^{-\Delta / T_k}, \qquad T_k = T_0\, e^{-\alpha k}
$$

so a high temperature early on allows exploration, and the search settles as the temperature falls. A smaller
$\alpha$ cools more slowly.

<More>
The epsilon-greedy bandit explores with probability $\varepsilon$, trying a random channel, and otherwise exploits,
choosing the channel that has been free most often so far. A channel earns a reward of 1 when it is free and 0 when
it is busy, so a larger $\varepsilon$ learns more about every channel at the cost of using the best one less.
</More>

## Results

The slowest cooling I tried gave the shortest tours, and more training data made the bandit's choice of channel more
reliable: with half the trace for training, its chosen channel was free 99.3% of the time.
