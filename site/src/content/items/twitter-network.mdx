## Problem

Collect real social-network data with our own crawler, and describe the structure of the network it produces.

## Data

245 accounts and 244 follower links, gathered two levels out from one public starting account.

<More>
The crawler took up to 50 followers for each account and expanded 12 accounts in all. Account names are real people's
and are not shown here.
</More>

## Approach

<Flow
  label="From the Twitter API to network measures"
  steps={['Crawl followers (tweepy)', 'Save to CSV', 'Build a directed edge list', 'Load into Gephi', 'Measure structure and centrality']}
/>

The crawler, written with tweepy, pages through each account's followers within the API's rate limits. Its output
becomes a directed edge list for Gephi.

## Calculations

PageRank scores an account by the importance of the accounts that follow it:

$$
PR(v) = \frac{1-d}{N} + d \sum_{u \to v} \frac{PR(u)}{\text{out}(u)}
$$

with damping factor $d = 0.85$, where $N$ is the number of accounts and $\text{out}(u)$ the number of accounts that
$u$ follows.

## Results

The network is a hub-and-spoke tree, and PageRank concentrates on the starting account.

| Measure | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Diameter | 2 |
| Average path length | 1.44 steps |
| PageRank of the starting account | 0.310 |
| Clustering coefficient | 0 |

<More>
Gephi also gave in-degree, out-degree, eccentricity, closeness and betweenness for every account, and I plotted the
degree distributions with pandas and matplotlib. A clustering coefficient of 0 is what to expect from a crawl that
expands outward from one account without following links back.
</More>
