## Context

Students on tight budgets need textbooks, while other students have books they no longer need. The product brief set
out a marketplace for Illinois Tech students to buy, sell, rent or exchange physical books, with buyer and seller
agreeing payment and handover between themselves. A six-person team developed it in Django as an interprofessional
course project.

## Approach

The team worked in weekly cycles on a Kanban board. The main systems were the catalogue, listings, accounts and
messaging, with a wishlist and fraud reporting as secondary features. Each function sat in a separate part of the
Django project, and every messaging and upload page required a signed-in user.

<More label="Team process and project structure">
- **Board and stories.** Work was tracked as numbered user stories on a Kanban board. Faculty advice during the term
  led to detailed descriptions and acceptance checklists on each story, and to stories taken on in pairs.
- **Weekly rhythm.** Each week a facilitator opened the board, and each member described the story completed and the
  one in progress. A working agreement set GitHub for shared code, email and Discord for communication, and a fixed
  weekend slot for extra meetings.
- **Progress reports.** Presenting rotated in pairs of consecutive weeks across the six members.
- **Structure.** The Django project has separate apps for the catalogue, listings, user registration, messaging and the
  landing page. The wishlist and fraud reporting sit inside the catalogue and registration apps.
</More>

## Contribution

- Developed the messaging system, in which two signed-in students hold one stored conversation and exchange messages
  to arrange payment and handover.
- Linked every listing to the account that posted it, so the catalogue shows the poster, and restricted uploads to
  signed-in users.
- Presented the team's progress in two weekly reports.

<More label="Messaging design and access checks">
- **Data model.** A conversation links two users. A message records its conversation, sender, receiver, text of up to
  1,000 characters and the time it was sent.
- **One thread per pair.** Starting a conversation by username first looks for an existing conversation between the
  two users in either order and reopens it, so each pair of students keeps a single thread whoever writes first.
- **Receiver.** When a message is sent, the receiver is taken from the conversation as the participant other than the
  sender, so no one enters a recipient twice.
- **Conversation list.** The Messages page lists only the conversations in which the signed-in user takes part,
  labelled with the other student's username.
- **Sign-in checks.** The conversation, message and upload views redirect to the login page when the user is not
  signed in.
- **Listing link.** Each catalogue listing holds a required reference to the user who posted it, and the upload view
  fills it from the signed-in user.
</More>

## Lessons learned

- **Define done before starting.** Faculty feedback on thin story descriptions led the team to add acceptance
  checklists, so that each story stated what finished work looked like.
- **Key a conversation by its pair of users.** Reusing one stored thread per pair kept the Messages list short and
  avoided duplicate conversations.
