## Problem

Fake news spreads through headlines that repeat or contradict each other. Given a headline already known to be fake and
a new one, can a model tell whether the new headline agrees with it, disagrees with it, or is unrelated?

## Data

256,442 labelled pairs of English news headlines, and 64,110 unlabelled pairs to predict. The labels are very uneven.

## Approach

<Flow
  label="The classification pipeline"
  steps={['Clean and lowercase', 'Tokenise and lemmatise (NLTK)', 'Word counts per headline pair', 'Scale features', 'Compare four models by five-fold cross-validation', 'Refit the best on all data and label the test set']}
/>

We compared logistic regression, a random forest, a decision tree and a linear support vector machine, each scored by
five-fold cross-validation.

<More>
The tokeniser runs inside the word-count step, so cleaning and counting stay in one pipeline. The features are scaled
without centring, which keeps the large word-count matrix sparse and fast to train on.
</More>

## Results

The random forest was the most accurate and the most stable of the four: 0.761 mean cross-validated accuracy,
varying by only ±0.002 across the five folds.
