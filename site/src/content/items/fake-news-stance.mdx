## Context

Fake news spreads through headlines that repeat or contradict each other. Given a headline already known to be fake and
a new one, the task was to decide whether the new headline agrees with it, disagrees with it, or is unrelated. The
labels were heavily uneven, so accuracy has to be read against a baseline.

<More label="Why the baseline matters">
Unrelated pairs are 68.5% of the labelled data, so a model that always answered "unrelated" would be right 68.5% of
the time. Every accuracy on this page is read against that figure.
</More>

## Approach

A text pipeline turned each headline pair into word counts, and four classifiers were compared by five-fold
cross-validation. The best of the four was refitted on all labelled pairs and used to label the unlabelled set.

<Flow
  label="The classification pipeline"
  steps={['Clean and lowercase', 'Tokenise and lemmatise (NLTK)', 'Word counts per headline pair', 'Scale features', 'Compare four models by five-fold cross-validation', 'Refit the best on all data and label the test set']}
/>

<More label="Pipeline and model details">
- Both headlines of a pair were joined into one text before counting, so each pair became a single vector of word
  counts.
- The tokeniser runs inside the word-count step, so cleaning and counting stay in one pipeline.
- Features were scaled without centring, which keeps the large word-count matrix sparse and fast to train on.
- The four models were logistic regression, a random forest, a decision tree and a linear support vector machine, all
  with scikit-learn's default settings.
</More>

## Findings and limitations

In the original 2023 procedure, the random forest reached 0.761 mean cross-validated accuracy, 7.6 percentage points
above the 68.5% majority-class baseline, and was the most accurate of the four models.

A re-evaluation in October 2026 fitted the word counts and scaler inside each training fold, on the same fold scheme as
in 2023 (five unshuffled stratified folds), so that only the preprocessing changed.
The original accuracies reproduced to within 0.2 points, and the correction changed them by at most 0.25 points, so
the leakage was negligible. Per-class results showed the larger weakness: the random forest found 6.7% of the
disagreeing pairs, and the decision tree had the highest macro F1.

<More label="Re-evaluation, October 2026: procedure and results">
- **Original setup:** the word-count vocabulary and the scaler were fitted on all 256,442 labelled pairs, then the four
  models were compared on five unshuffled stratified folds. The pairs in each test fold had therefore influenced the
  features.
- **Corrected procedure:** the same text handling and the same four models with default settings, with the word counts
  and scaler fitted on the four training folds only, on the same five unshuffled folds, so that only the preprocessing
  changed. The majority-class baseline was scored on
  the same folds, with per-class precision, recall and F1.
- **Corrected accuracy:** random forest 76.1% (SD 0.3), decision tree 70.2% (0.7), logistic regression 67.5% (0.9),
  linear support vector machine 64.6% (0.7), against the 68.5% baseline. The original procedure, rerun on the same
  folds, scored 0.05 to 0.25 points higher: this is the measured size of the leakage, smaller than each model's
  variation across folds.
- **Disagreeing pairs:** the random forest found 440 of the 6,606 (recall 6.7%, precision 64.2%, F1 12.0%). The
  decision tree found 21.2% of them, at 26.2% precision and 23.3% F1.
- **Repeated headlines:** the same headline often appears in several pairs, and 65.3% of test pairs shared a headline
  with their training folds. Shuffling the folds raises that share to 98.4% and the random forest's accuracy to 85.3%,
  which suggests repeated headlines affect the scores far more than the preprocessing did.
- **What changed in the interpretation:** the 2023 accuracies stand as cross-validation estimates. The random forest's
  lead over the baseline comes from the unrelated pairs, two of the four models score below the baseline, and on the
  rare disagreeing pairs the decision tree is the strongest of the four.
</More>

<More label="Variation across folds and limitations">
- The report gives each accuracy with the standard deviation of the five fold accuracies: random forest 0.002, decision
  tree 0.007, logistic regression 0.008, linear support vector machine 0.006.
- About two thirds of test pairs shared a headline with their training folds, so the scores may be optimistic for
  headlines never seen before; that effect has not been measured, and measuring it needs folds split by story.
- Counting the words of both headlines together ignores which words came from which headline, and no model settings
  were tuned.
- The unlabelled pairs came without labels, so the accuracy is a cross-validation estimate, not a test-set score.
</More>
