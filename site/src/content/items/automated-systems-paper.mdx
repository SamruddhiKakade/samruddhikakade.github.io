## Question

How can software be developed to be reliable enough for automated systems in mission-critical environments, where an
error can mean major financial loss or loss of life?

## Argument

Automated systems replace work done by people and run with minimal human intervention, so the paper treated extreme
reliability as their defining requirement. It surveyed how software development evolved to meet this, from the rigid
Waterfall model to the iterative Agile model, and named three threats to reliability: requirements that change or are
misread, inadequate testing, and keeping software current with changing technology after deployment.

The case study followed E-ZPass from manual to automatic toll collection: early tests of automatic vehicle
identification, a committee of seven agencies in 1991, and deployment at the Spring Valley toll plaza on the Thruway in
1993. For greater reliability, the paper proposed clear requirements that are checked iteratively, extensive testing,
stress testing, and an automated fail-safe that records passing vehicles during a software fault so that they can be
charged through other means.
