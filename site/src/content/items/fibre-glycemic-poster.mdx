## Question

Does adding extracted plant fibre to a cereal bar lower the rise in blood glucose and insulin after eating it?

## The study

A double-blind, randomised, crossover trial in ten healthy adults aged 20 to 40: each person ate the cereal bar both
with and without added fibre, on separate visits, and their glucose and insulin were measured over the next two hours.

<More label="Trial design and the two bars">
The trial was approved by an institutional review board and run by a research team; the course supplied its data and a
written description of the study. The two bars matched in energy (242 kcal), fat and protein, and differed in fibre:
none in one bar and 5 g of plant-extracted fibre in the other. Participants fasted before each visit, with blood
samples taken before eating and at 30, 60 and 120 minutes. The poster set out the background, objective, methods,
results and conclusion, with tables of the participants' characteristics and the composition of the two bars.
</More>

## Results

On average, the bar with added fibre gave a lower glucose and insulin response than the bar without it. Both peaked at
30 minutes, and the 30-minute concentrations were lower with fibre.

Over the full two hours, the total area under each curve was 11% lower for glucose and 27% lower for insulin with
fibre. In a paired t-test across the ten participants, both differences were significant (glucose p = 0.002, insulin
p < 0.001). The insulin area was lower with fibre in all ten participants and the glucose area in nine.

## Findings and limitations

The crossover design compares each person with themselves, which removes differences between people from the result.
The trial was small and short: ten participants and a two-hour window, with one fibre bar.

<More label="Analysis method and caveats">
The area under each curve was calculated with the trapezoid rule on the four samples per visit, using total
concentration rather than the rise above baseline. At 120 minutes the mean insulin concentration was higher with fibre
(18.8 against 12.8 µIU/mL), mostly because of one participant's value on the fibre visit; the area measure, which
covers the whole two hours, still favoured fibre in every participant. The data were supplied for the course, so the
results describe this dataset and are not offered as a new finding.
</More>
