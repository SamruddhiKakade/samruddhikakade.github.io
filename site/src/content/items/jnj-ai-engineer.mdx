## Problem

An enterprise chatbot is only as good as the knowledge it draws on. Healthcare knowledge comes from many sources, in
many shapes, and every answer has to meet regulatory and governance standards.

<More>
When sources are duplicated, inconsistent or quietly change their structure over time, a retrieval-augmented chatbot
finds the wrong passage, or several conflicting ones. Its answers become slower, less relevant and harder to trust.
Much of the quality of such a system is decided before the language model ever sees a question.
</More>

## Approach

<Flow
  label="The general pattern of the pipelines I work on"
  steps={['Knowledge sources', 'Ingestion', 'Cleaning and quality checks', 'Metadata and semantic categories', 'Retrieval index', 'Chatbot answers']}
/>

I build the stages between the sources and the chatbot, so that each passage arrives clean and labelled.

<More>
- **Metadata and semantic categories.** Each piece of knowledge is described and grouped by meaning, so retrieval can
  narrow its search to the right part of the collection before ranking passages.
- **Quality checks.** Redundancy, inconsistency and schema drift are caught and corrected before they reach the
  chatbot, with corrective workflows rather than one-off fixes.
</More>

## My part

- I build and maintain the ingestion pipelines.
- I design the metadata and semantic categorisation.
- I diagnose data-quality problems and put corrective workflows in place.
- I work with product, compliance, governance and engineering teams.

<More>
- Turning unclear needs into clear functional and data requirements.
- Explaining technical results to business stakeholders in plain terms.
- Supporting live use of the chatbots and improving them from user and stakeholder feedback.
- Working within compliance and governance requirements in a regulated healthcare environment.
</More>

## What I learned

Working on AI in production has changed how I think about building it:

- **The data decides the answer.** Answers come from what the chatbot retrieves, so clean, well-described sources
  improve them more reliably than changes made further down the line.
- **Structure is part of retrieval.** How knowledge is described and grouped decides what the chatbot can find, so
  I treat metadata and categories as part of the design.

<More>
- **Fix the cause.** When a data problem appears once, it will appear again, so I build corrective workflows that
  catch the whole class of problem.
- **Compliance is a design input.** In a regulated setting, bringing compliance and governance in early, and
  documenting decisions as I make them, means a solution is ready for production when it is ready technically.
- **Clear requirements save the most time.** Turning a loosely stated need into precise functional and data
  requirements, and explaining results to business stakeholders in plain terms, keeps everyone building toward the
  same thing.
- **Live use is the real test.** Watching how a system is used and folding feedback into the next change teaches me
  more than any test run before launch.
</More>
