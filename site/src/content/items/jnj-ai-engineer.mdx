## Context

An enterprise chatbot depends on the knowledge it retrieves. Healthcare knowledge comes from many sources and in many
formats, and the pipelines that prepare it operate within healthcare compliance and governance requirements.

<More label="How source quality affects retrieval">
When sources are duplicated, inconsistent or change their structure over time, a retrieval-augmented chatbot can
retrieve the wrong passage, or several conflicting ones, and its answers become less relevant. In these systems, data
quality and metadata had a direct effect on retrieval.
</More>

## Approach

<Flow
  label="The general pattern of the pipelines"
  steps={['Knowledge sources', 'Cleaning and quality checks', 'Metadata and semantic categories', 'Ingestion', 'Retrieval index', 'Chatbot answers']}
/>

Each source is checked, described and categorised before ingestion, so that every passage reaches the retrieval index
clean and labelled.

<More label="Metadata, categories and quality checks">
- **Metadata and semantic categories.** Each piece of knowledge is described and grouped by meaning, so retrieval can
  narrow its search to the right part of the collection before ranking passages.
- **Quality checks and corrective workflows.** Checks detect redundancy, inconsistency and schema drift. When a check
  finds a problem, a corrective workflow resolves that class of problem, not only the single instance.
</More>

## Contribution

- Evaluating several approved models for the project, which informed the choice of model.
- Collaborating with product, compliance, governance and engineering teams in a regulated healthcare environment.

<More label="Requirements, communication and live support">
- Turning unclear needs into clear functional and data requirements.

</More>

## Lessons learned

- **Data quality and metadata matter for retrieval.** The passages a chatbot retrieved depended on how clean and well
  described its sources were.
- **Fix the cause.** A data problem that appears once is likely to appear again, so corrective workflows address the
  whole class of problem.

<More label="Further lessons: compliance, requirements and live use">
- **Compliance is a design input.** Involving compliance and governance teams early, and documenting decisions as they
  are made, avoids waiting on approval once a solution is technically ready.
- **Clear requirements keep teams aligned.** Turning a loosely stated need into precise functional and data
  requirements, and explaining results to business stakeholders in plain terms, keeps teams working from the same
  understanding.
- **Live use shows what testing does not.** Watching how a system is used and feeding that back into the next change
  surfaces problems that pre-launch testing did not.
</More>
