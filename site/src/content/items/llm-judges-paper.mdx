## Question

Systems that answer questions from contracts, regulations or standards can add a second model, an LLM judge, to check
each answer before a user sees it. The judge is often given an amendment record saying which document replaced which.
Such records are rarely complete, and where a record is silent it effectively reports a replaced document as current.
The paper asks what the judge relies on when the record and the documents disagree.

## Approach

The test starts from any table of replacements. For each replacement it writes one answer that cites the replaced
document, the fault, and three answers that are correct. It then removes half of the links from the record, presents the
damaged record in several ways that leave its content unchanged, and counts a fault as caught only when the judge says
the cited document was replaced.

<More label="Corpus, judges and the matched controls">
- Corpus: 48 replacements recorded by the RFC Editor of the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF), chosen because the
  publisher keeps an exact record.
- Judges: Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.5 and the open-weight Gemma 4 26B.
- The three correct answers per replacement: one citing the successor; a date control, citing the replaced document at
  a date before the replacement existed; and a decoy, citing an older document that a newer one updated without
  replacing it.
- Scoring by stated reason: a judge that flags every older citation, or any older document next to a newer one on the
  same subject, would score well on detection without checking currency. Counting a catch only for the right reason
  separates the two.
- The hypotheses, tests and how each outcome would be read were written after a smaller pilot and committed to the
  project's own repository before the main runs: an internal, timestamped record rather than a public registration.
</More>

## Findings and limitations

With a correct record, the judges caught nearly every replaced citation, as a lookup in the record would. On the 24
faults whose link was removed, every judge caught more from the texts alone than with the record present, and the drop
recurred on a second random draw of removed links. Most missed faults were passed without any finding.

<More label="Results by judge and by presentation">
- Faults caught from the texts alone against with the damaged record: Gemini 3.1 Pro 23 of 24 against 1 of 24, GPT-5.5
  23 of 24 against 9 of 24, Claude Opus 4.5 17 of 24 against 9 of 24, and Gemma 4 26B 13 of 24 against 0 of 24.
- Rewording the gap as "no replacement recorded", a true statement, did not restore the lost catches.
- Presenting the record as an unverified note, or warning that it was incomplete, restored most of the lost catches for
  Gemini, GPT-5.5 and Claude, significant after correction for the first two, but at most 5 of 13 for Gemma. Where
  catches returned, so did false alarms on updates.
- In an exploratory run that offered a reason code for a record that conflicts with the evidence, only Gemini used it
  often.
- Given both texts and no record, the judges mostly told replacements from updates, but every update they flagged had a
  near-identical title.
</More>

<More label="Limitations">
- One corpus of technical standards and four judges. The answer is limited to these judges, prompts and documents, not
  LLM judges in general, and the study cannot say why the judges behaved as they did.
- The gap result rests on 36 distinct links over two draws of 24, and the hosted judges ran once per prompt.
- The evidence is titles, dates and abstracts, not full documents, and links were removed at random, while real records
  fail in patterns.
- The case study on Indian rooftop-solar rules is descriptive: its answer key did not pass the paper's own quality
  checks, and nothing in the main results rests on it.
</More>
