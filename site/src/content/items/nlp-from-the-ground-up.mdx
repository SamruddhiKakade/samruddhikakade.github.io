## Problem

How different are two collections of text, and how far can simple probabilistic models go at classifying text?

## Data

- A sentiment corpus of movie-review sentences (SST) and a question-answering corpus (QNLI), for comparing corpora
  and for sentiment classification.
- A set of news headlines labelled as clickbait or not.

## Approach

1. **Comparing corpora.** I wrote entropy and KL divergence from scratch, over the word distributions of the two
   corpora.
2. **Clickbait detection.** A Naive Bayes classifier on word counts, tuned by grid search, compared with a rule-based
   classifier built from the words most indicative of clickbait.
3. **Sentiment from language models.** A bigram language model with add-α smoothing, written in Python. I trained
   one model per sentiment class and labelled each sentence with the class whose model found it most likely.

## Calculations

$$
H(P) = -\sum_{w} P(w)\log_2 P(w)
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\,\Vert\,Q) = \sum_{w} P(w)\log_2\frac{P(w)}{Q(w)}
$$

<More>
The bigram model estimates the probability of each word given the one before it, with add-α smoothing so unseen
pairs are not impossible:

$$
P(w_i \mid w_{i-1}) = \frac{\text{count}(w_{i-1}, w_i) + \alpha}{\text{count}(w_{i-1}) + \alpha\,|V|}
$$

I chose α on a validation set: 0.001 fit best of the values I tried (0.001, 0.01 and 0.1).
</More>

## Results

- The two corpora have similar word entropy, but KL divergence is clearly asymmetric (table below).
- The bigram language-model classifier labelled 90% of held-out sentences correctly.
- Naive Bayes found far more of the clickbait than the word rules, at similar precision.
