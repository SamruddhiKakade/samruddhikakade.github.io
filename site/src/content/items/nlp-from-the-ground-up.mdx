## Context

Three assignments in CS 585 Natural Language Processing (Fall 2023) asked how different two collections of text are,
and how far simple probabilistic models can classify text. Each used a different dataset:

- The binary Stanford Sentiment Treebank (SST-2): its training file of 67,349 labelled movie-review phrases, used to
  compare corpora and, split at random, to classify sentiment.
- The development set of QNLI: 5,463 Wikipedia sentences, used as the second corpus.
- 2,388 news headlines labelled as clickbait (814) or not (1,574).

## Approach

Three analyses, each written in Python:

1. Word entropy for each corpus, and KL divergence between them in both directions.
2. A Naive Bayes clickbait classifier on word counts, tuned by grid search, compared with a five-word rule classifier
   and with labelling every headline clickbait.
3. A bigram language model with add-α smoothing for sentiment: one model per class, with each phrase assigned to the
   class whose model, weighted by the class prior, found it more likely.

<More label="Formulae, smoothing and tuning settings">
$$
H(P) = -\sum_{w} P(w)\log_2 P(w)
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\,\Vert\,Q) = \sum_{w} P(w)\log_2\frac{P(w)}{Q(w)}
$$

A word absent from the other corpus was given a probability of 0.0001, since the divergence is otherwise infinite.

The bigram model estimates the probability of each word given the one before it, with add-α smoothing so that unseen
pairs are not impossible:

$$
P(w_i \mid w_{i-1}) = \frac{\text{count}(w_{i-1}, w_i) + \alpha}{\text{count}(w_{i-1}) + \alpha\,|V|}
$$

The value of α was chosen on 100 validation phrases from 0.001, 0.01 and 0.1; 0.001 gave the highest log-likelihood.

The clickbait headlines were split 1,719 for training, 191 for validation and 478 for testing. The grid search covered
18 combinations of document-frequency limit, smoothing value and word or word-pair features, and the combination with
the best validation F1 was used on the test set. The rule classifier used the five words with the highest clickbait
log-probability: believe, won, just, new and thing.
</More>

## Contribution

- Implemented word entropy and KL divergence in Python over the word distributions of two corpora.
- Trained a Naive Bayes clickbait classifier in scikit-learn, tuned it by grid search on a validation set, derived a
  five-word rule classifier from its most indicative words and compared both with an always-clickbait baseline.
- Implemented a bigram language model with add-α smoothing, selected α on a validation set and classified sentiment
  with one model per class and class priors.

## Findings and limitations

The bigram classifier labelled 90 of 100 held-out phrases correctly, against 56 of 100 for always answering positive.

<More label="Limitations of the evaluation">
- The sentiment test set is 100 phrases, so the 90% carries an uncertainty of roughly 6 percentage points either way.
- The held-out phrases were drawn at random from the SST-2 training file rather than the official development or test
  split, and SST-2 phrases overlap with one another, so the 90% is not comparable with published SST-2 results.
- α was chosen from three values and 0.001 was the smallest, so a smaller value might fit better.
- The two corpora differ in size (about 599,000 words from SST-2 and 155,000 from QNLI), which affects both entropy and
  divergence, and the divergence depends on the probability given to missing words.
- The clickbait result is one train and test split, shuffled without a fixed seed, so a rerun gives slightly different
  figures.
</More>
