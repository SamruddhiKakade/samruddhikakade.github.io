## Problem

Diners want quick answers about a menu and an easy way to order, by voice or by text. Every restaurant has its own
menu and setup, so one assistant has to serve many restaurants.

## Approach

<Flow
  label="How a customer's question becomes an answer or an order"
  steps={['Chat or voice in the browser', 'Speech to text (Azure Speech)', 'Flask service', "Azure OpenAI, answering from the restaurant's live menu", 'Reply as text or speech', "Order sent to the restaurant's ordering system"]}
/>

The assistant answers from the restaurant's current menu, keeps track of the conversation, and turns a confirmed
order into a structured order for the restaurant.

<More>
- **Prompt design.** A persona, rules for the conversation, an order format with a worked example, and the
  restaurant's menu, assembled for each restaurant when a session starts.
- **Voice.** Speech-to-text for questions and text-to-speech for answers, with short spoken summaries of long replies.
- **Retrieval.** Knowledge bases for retrieval, and prompts and retrieval tuned so answers stay grounded in the menu.
- **Many restaurants.** Settings for each restaurant are kept in MongoDB, and each restaurant reaches the service
  with its own access token.
</More>

## How it grew

1. A single-restaurant demo with a fixed menu.
2. Live menus fetched from the restaurant platform.
3. A multi-restaurant service with per-restaurant settings and secure access.

<More>
Each version kept what worked and removed a limit of the one before: first the fixed menu, then the single
restaurant. The last version separated configuration from code, so adding a restaurant meant adding data rather
than writing code.
</More>

## Calculations

To help choose a provider, I built a cost model for running the assistant:

$$
\text{cost per conversation} = n_{\text{in}}\cdot p_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}}\cdot p_{\text{out}}
\;\;(+\ \text{hourly hosting, for a fine-tuned model})
$$

where $n$ is the number of input or output tokens and $p$ the price per token.

<More>
I compared two providers (Azure OpenAI and OpenAI's own platform) on three costs: fine-tuning a model, running each
conversation, and hosting a fine-tuned model. I then rolled them up into the cost of a fixed number of conversations,
so the choice could be made on the total rather than on any single price.
</More>
